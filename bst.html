      Keyboard shortcuts when focus is outside a control or scroll region: Space play/pause, ←/→ step, Home reset.

      BST Ordering Invariant

      Every key in a node’s left subtree is strictly smaller than the node’s key, and every key in its right subtree is strictly larger—across all ancestors, not only the immediate parent. Keys are unique.

      Three Delete Cases & In-Order Successor

      Deleting a leaf unlinks it; deleting a one-child node promotes its child; deleting a two-child node copies the in-order successor (minimum key in the right subtree) into the target and relinks the successor’s right child.

      Four Tree Traversals

      In-Order (L → Root → R) yields sorted ascending keys; Pre-Order (Root → L → R) captures tree structure; Post-Order (L → R → Root) visits children before parents; Level-Order (BFS) walks level by level with a queue.

      Balanced O(log n) vs. Skewed O(n)

      Search, insert, delete, and min/max take O(h) time, where h is tree height (empty −1, single node 0). Balanced inputs keep h ≈ log₂ n, whereas inserting sorted keys forms a skewed chain of height n − 1.
