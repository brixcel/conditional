        Trie Complexity & Prefix-Tree Properties

        Operation Complexity Notes

        Keyboard shortcuts when focus is outside an input, control, or scroll region: Space play/pause, ←/→ step, Home reset.

        Shared Prefixes & Edge Letters

        Each downward edge carries one character (a–z), with sibling edges ordered alphabetically, and the root stands for the empty prefix ∅. Words with a common beginning—such as “car”, “card”, and “care”—share the path c → a → r instead of duplicating those letters.

        Why isEnd Markers Matter (“car” vs “card”)

        Reaching a node only proves that its path is a prefix of some stored word. A word-end marker (isEnd === true, shown as a double ring with ★) tells search(“car”) that “car” itself was inserted, while search(“ca”) returns not found even though startsWith(“ca”) succeeds.

        Bottom-Up Pruning Stop Rules

        Deletion clears isEnd and then walks upward, pruning unused nodes. It stops immediately at a node with remaining children or another word’s isEnd, producing three outcomes: unmark-only (e.g. delete “car”), partial-prune (e.g. delete “card” or “door”), or full-prune all the way to root (e.g. delete “bat”).

        Why Lookup Is O(L) Independent of Dictionary Size N

        At each node, stepping to the next character edge takes O(1) time over the fixed 26-letter alphabet. Therefore, insert, search, startsWith, and delete each take O(L) time for word or prefix length L (at most L downward edges, plus up to L upward pruning steps on delete), whether the trie stores 10 words or 1,000,000 words.
