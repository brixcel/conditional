Red-Black Tree

The five red-black properties

  1. Every node is either red or black.
  2. The root is black.
  3. Every leaf (NIL, an empty child pointer) counts as black.
  4. A red node has only black children: two reds are never parent and child.
  5. For every node, all paths from it down to a NIL leaf pass the same number of black nodes (its black height).

Rules 4 and 5 together mean the longest root-to-leaf path is at most twice the shortest, so a tree with n keys has height at most 2·log₂(n + 1). Search, insert and delete are therefore O(log n). An insert needs at most 2 rotations and a delete at most 3; recolouring may repeat up the tree, but only O(log n) times.

      Red-Black Tree Complexity & Fix-Up Cases

      Operation Time Notes

      Keyboard shortcuts when focus is outside a control or scroll region: Space play/pause, ←/→ step, Home reset. Each case below also has a mirror image (left and right swapped); the demos show both sides.

      Insert Case 1: Uncle Red → Recolour

      The new red node z has a red parent, and the parent's sibling (the uncle) is also red. Colour the parent and uncle black and the grandparent red. No rotation; the check repeats from the grandparent, which may now clash with its own parent.

      Insert Case 2: Uncle Black, Triangle

      The uncle is black (or NIL) and z is an inner child (a left child of a right child, or the mirror). Rotate the parent away from z so the three nodes form a straight line; this turns it into case 3.

      Insert Case 3: Uncle Black, Line

      The uncle is black and z, its parent and grandparent lie in a straight line. Rotate the grandparent towards the uncle, then swap the colours of the old parent (now black) and old grandparent (now red). The fix-up ends.

      Delete: Where the Double Black Comes From

      Removing a black node leaves its paths one black short. If the node that takes its place (x) is red, colour it black and stop. Otherwise x, possibly an empty NIL child, is treated as double black and one of the four sibling (w) cases below pushes the extra black away.

      Delete Case 1: Sibling Red

      Colour the sibling w black and the parent red, then rotate the parent towards x. x now has a black sibling, so case 2, 3 or 4 follows.

      Delete Case 2: Sibling Black, Both Its Children Black

      Colour w red, taking one black off both sides, and move the double black up to the parent. A red parent simply turns black and the fix-up ends; at the root the extra black is dropped.

      Delete Case 3: Sibling Black, Near Child Red, Far Child Black

      Swap the colours of w and its near child, then rotate w away from x. The new sibling is black with a red far child: case 4.

      Delete Case 4: Sibling Black, Far Child Red

      w takes the parent's colour; the parent and the far child become black; then rotate the parent towards x. The extra black is absorbed and the fix-up ends.
