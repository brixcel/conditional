AVL Tree Visualization

A height-balanced Binary Search Tree named after Adelson-Velsky and Landis. Every node stores its height; its balance factor (height of left subtree − height of right subtree) must stay −1, 0 or +1. Step through each insert and delete: watch heights update on the way back up, see the tree in its unbalanced state, then the rotation (LL, RR, LR or RL) that repairs it.

      AVL Tree Complexity & Rotation Cases

      Operation Time Notes

      Keyboard shortcuts when focus is outside a control or scroll region: Space play/pause, ←/→ step, Home reset.

      Left-Left (LL) → Right Rotation

      A node has balance factor +2 and its left child has balance factor +1 (or 0 after a delete). One right rotation around the node lifts the left child into its place.

      Right-Right (RR) → Left Rotation

      A node has balance factor −2 and its right child has balance factor −1 (or 0 after a delete). One left rotation around the node lifts the right child into its place.

      Left-Right (LR) Double Rotation

      A node has balance factor +2 but its left child leans right (balance factor −1). First a left rotation on the child turns it into the LL case, then a right rotation on the node.

      Right-Left (RL) Double Rotation

      A node has balance factor −2 but its right child leans left (balance factor +1). First a right rotation on the child turns it into the RR case, then a left rotation on the node.
